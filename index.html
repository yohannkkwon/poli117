The compliance problem

A plan that passes by a rounding error is not a plan that passes.

Senate Bill 375 requires the San Diego region to cut per-capita greenhouse gas emissions from cars and light-duty trucks 19% below 2005 levels by 2035. The 2005 baseline is 26 pounds of CO₂ per person per weekday.

SANDAG's 2025 Regional Plan, adopted in December 2025, is projected to deliver 19.35%. The 2021 Regional Plan projected 20%. The margin is shrinking, not growing, and every assumption inside it has to hold for a decade.

Meanwhile the region removed its four-cents-per-mile road usage charge in December 2021, leaving part of the funding structure unresolved.

Where it comes from

Transportation isn't one sector among many. It's the whole problem.

San Diego's 2016 regional greenhouse gas inventory. On-road vehicles account for 48% of emissions, more than electricity, natural gas, and industry combined.

Who pays for it

The people driving least are absorbing the most.

Car dependence is not distributed evenly, and neither is the pollution it creates. The neighborhoods with the least transit access sit closest to the freeways carrying everyone else's commute.

The two levers

Clean the cars that remain. Shrink the miles we have to drive.

Neither lever is sufficient alone. Electrification cannot outrun rising vehicle miles traveled, and transit cannot be built fast enough to carry a region this spread out. Pulled together, they attack the problem from both ends.

Lever 01 · Vehicles

Equitable electrification

Convert electric vehicle incentives into income-tiered, point-of-sale rebates, and aim charging investment at multifamily housing and disadvantaged communities rather than letting market demand decide where it lands.

  • Rebate applied at purchase, not at tax filing, scaled by household income
  • Charging build-out prioritized by a published equity map, not by demand
  • Funded from existing streams: California directs ≥35% of cap-and-trade proceeds to disadvantaged communities; federal Justice40 commits 40%
Lever 02 · Mobility

Reduce the need to drive

Sequence transit corridors and Mobility Hubs together with rezoning and affordable housing, so new homes arrive with the transit that serves them, and capture the short trips that need no car at all.

  • Transit Leap corridors phased alongside transit-oriented affordable housing
  • Complete Streets and micromobility in Mobility Hubs
  • Nearly half of all regional trips are three miles or less, the most replaceable miles we have

A note on scale. San Diego produces a small fraction of global emissions. The case for acting here is not that the region can solve the problem alone. It is that California's regions function as living laboratories, and that the health burden of these emissions falls locally, on people who are already breathing them.
